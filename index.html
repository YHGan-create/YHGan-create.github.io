National University of Singapore · Molecular microbiology

Understanding the biology
of bacterial infection.

At the National University of Singapore, we investigate how bacterial pathogens colonise, cause disease and exchange antimicrobial resistance — from molecular mechanisms to infection biology.

Since about 2015, our laboratory has been studying hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae and Klebsiella-induced liver abscess, motivated by the high incidence of this disease in parts of Asia, including Singapore. We have identified different patterns of infection among ethnic groups in Singapore, with certain lineages of hypervirulent K. pneumoniae associated predominantly with the Chinese population. Our ongoing studies investigate how the hypermucoid capsule of the K1 lineage, iron siderophores, colibactin and microcin loci contribute to different stages of disease.

We have expanded our research to examine the transmission of antibiotic resistance in carbapenem-resistant K. pneumoniae, in response to the growing global threat of antimicrobial resistance. In its 2017 global priority list, the World Health Organization classified carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales as critical-priority pathogens. We hypothesize that successful clinical resistance plasmids have distinctive adaptations and properties, including interactions with their preferred bacterial hosts. Our goal is to identify these properties and interactions and use that knowledge to develop strategies that limit resistance transmission.

We are also investigating the gut as a reservoir for bacterial pathogens and exploring approaches to effectively decolonize these bacteria from the intestinal tract.
